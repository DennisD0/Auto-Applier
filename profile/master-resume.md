## The three filters — apply before anything ships

### 1. Interview defensibility

**If Dennis cannot defend it in an interview, it does not appear anywhere.** This is the
primary filter and it outranks keyword-matching, ATS optimization, and score improvement. A
claim that wins a screen and collapses in a conversation is worse than no claim.

### 2. AI-builder honesty

**Dennis does not hand-write code.** He directs AI tools, primarily Claude Code, and in his
own words "mostly prompts and accepts." Coursework gave him light C++ and Java, which is not
interview-defensible.

Every technical claim must be scoped as **"directing AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) as
SWEs"** — never phrasing that implies he personally wrote the code. Naming the agents
specifically is a *differentiator* when anchored to a concrete shipped outcome, not a
liability. Vague "AI-assisted" language is weaker than the honest specific version.

The updated resume sharpens this considerably: the credible unit of work is **writing user
stories and managing Linear issues via MCP so agents can autonomously read, update, and enrich
tickets**. That is a real PM activity he can defend in an interview, and it converts the
"doesn't code" fact into the actual value proposition. Lead with it.

### 3. Metric honesty

Inflated-sounding figures get softened to modeled projections or scope-based framing. Small
engagements get realistic, defensible numbers. See the per-role notes below.
